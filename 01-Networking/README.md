# Module 1 — Networking Fundamentals

This module builds the networking knowledge needed for later security work.

## Learning outcomes
By the end of this module, I should be able to:
- Explain the OSI and TCP/IP models and relate them to real traffic.
- Identify IPv4 addresses, prefixes, gateways, DNS servers, and interfaces.
- Explain Ethernet, MAC addresses, ARP, switching, and routing.
- Distinguish TCP and UDP and describe ports and sockets.
- Explain DNS, DHCP, ICMP, HTTP, and HTTPS at a foundational level.
- Use basic diagnostics and capture traffic from my own lab.
- Write a reproducible report with observations and troubleshooting.

## Lesson sequence
1. Lab inventory and interface discovery
2. OSI and TCP/IP models
3. Bits, bytes, binary, and hexadecimal essentials
4. IPv4 addressing, subnet masks, and CIDR
5. Private/public addressing, loopback, and link-local
6. MAC addresses, Ethernet, ARP, and switching
7. Routing, default gateways, and NAT
8. TCP, UDP, ports, and sockets
9. DNS and DHCP
10. ICMP and network diagnostics
11. HTTP/HTTPS and TLS basics
12. Packet capture with Wireshark or tcpdump
13. Firewall and segmentation fundamentals
14. Integrated troubleshooting lab and review

## Lab safety
Use only your own machines, intentionally vulnerable training systems, or networks for which you have explicit permission. Do not scan public or third-party targets without authorisation. Sanitise IP addresses, hostnames, screenshots, and packet captures before publishing.

## Lab records
Create a separate report for each practical exercise under 05-Reports or in a clearly named lesson folder. Include commands, expected output, actual observations, troubleshooting, and conclusions.
